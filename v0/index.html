What's involved?

They ask how confident you feel about planning your informatics career and how useful the session was. They also send your menu choices from step 1 about your role and workplace (profession, career stage, informatics experience, share of time in informatics, type of organization, country, your role on each project, and what got in the way) and how you used the coach. Not your name, specialty, or anything you type in the steps. Each survey is sent once, when you press Send or move on. There's no name or email. A random code links your two surveys. We'll use the combined answers to improve the workshop and may share them in a presentation or publication. Taking part is voluntary, and you can skip any question.

Where to go next

Training and certification

  • Clinical Informatics board certification (physicians, ABPM). Requires an ACGME-accredited fellowship. The practice pathway is closed.
  • Find an accredited fellowship (ACGME program search, then choose Clinical Informatics) and the AMIA fellowship match.
  • AMIA Health Informatics Certification (AHIC) for nurses, pharmacists, other clinicians, and physicians who aren't board-certified in informatics.
  • Informatics Nursing certification (ANCC)
  • AMIA 10x10 courses: short online courses, a common starting point.
  • Informatics degree and certificate programs (AMIA directory)
  • Also useful: Lean or Six Sigma training, project management training, and your EHR vendor's builder training.

Communities

  • Physicians in AMIA (PINA)
  • AMIA Nursing Informatics
  • American Nursing Informatics Association (ANIA)
  • AMDIS (physician informatics leaders)
  • AMIA career development (includes the AMIA job board)

AI in clinical informatics

Clinical AI is a growing part of informatics work: choosing, evaluating, putting into use, and governing AI tools in care.

  • Cultivating AI leaders: the role of clinical informatics fellowship in transforming healthcare (Lingam et al., 2026; free full text)
  • AMIA 10x10: Introduction to Biomedical Informatics and AI (short online course)

The phenotypes

A phenotype is a common mix of informatics work, built from how often people do 20 kinds of work. Phenotypes overlap. They aren't personality types or validated categories. Use them to reflect, talk with mentors, and plan.

Limits: the survey and phenotypes come from physicians. Role names are written for all clinicians, but there are no nurse-, APP-, or pharmacist-specific phenotypes yet.

Sources and further reading

how the phenotypes were made, and 13 references

How the phenotypes were made

Informatics faculty and fellows drafted them from experience across several fellowship programs and from earlier ways of describing informatics work: the CI Domains of Practice1, KOI Pond7, and #MyInformaticsProfile8. About 100 informaticians answered a survey through Physicians in AMIA, a regional informatics group, and academic faculty groups (90 usable responses). The weights for each phenotype came from the survey and were adjusted by expert review12. They were first used with fellows at AMIA's Clinical Informatics Conference retreats in 2023 and 202512,13. This supports using them for reflection, not for classifying people.

Other ways to describe informatics work

  • Clinical Informatics Domains of Practice (physician subspecialty, 2019): 5 domains, 42 tasks, 139 knowledge and skill statements. The basis of the board exam.
  • Health Informatics Domains of Practice (all professions, 2020). The basis of AMIA's AHIC certification.
  • Clinical Informatics board exam content outline (ABPM)
  • ACGME Clinical Informatics Milestones (how fellows are assessed)
  • KOI Pond: describe your work by setting (internal, external, policy) and focus (knowledge, operations, innovation).
  • #MyInformaticsProfile: show where your time goes across areas of informatics.
